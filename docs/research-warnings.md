## Applicability and playback

[OSHA's forklift guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/powered-industrial-trucks/types-fundamentals/parts/warning-safety-devices) describes backup alarms as optional manufacturer equipment that sounds when a forklift reverses. [OSHA's backover guidance](https://www.osha.gov/preventing-backovers/standards) says US federal OSHA does not specifically require backup alarms on all powered industrial trucks and notes maritime rules against removing manufacturer-installed safety devices. Requirements vary with equipment, site and jurisdiction; these sources do not establish that every terminal machine sounds a reversing alarm. Model an alarm for a vehicle known or configured to have one when reverse is engaged, rather than triggering it for all movement. Use a vehicle-positioned point source and attenuate with distance; this is simulation guidance, not an operational safety specification. A repeated recording needs a separately reviewed loop boundary and should stop when the configured alarm stops.
