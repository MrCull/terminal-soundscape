# Source and candidate research log

Updated 2026-10-31. These are source-page checks, not listening reviews. Rights and properties are maintained per item in `manifest.json`; nothing here is marked release-ready.

- [WWS Mobile harbour crane](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WWS_Mobileharbourcrane.ogg): authentic Gottwald HMK7408B mobile cargo handling crane, Teemu Ahola, CC BY 4.0. Candidate because the item page records the equipment and licence; unauditioned.
- [WWS Container ship gantry crane](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WWS_Containershipgantrycrane.ogg): authentic gantry crane loading M/S Sandy Rickmers at Port of Rauma; Teemu Ahola; CC BY 4.0. Candidate because the source has detailed provenance and recorded reuse permission; unauditioned.

Source description says three gantry cranes transport containers and acoustic start signals are audible. Candidate for handling/terminal/warning research, but the sound details have not been heard.
- [WWS Container reach stackers](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WWS_Containerreachstackers.ogg): Teemu Ahola; CC BY 4.0. Source identifies Kone Cranes SMV 4531 TB5 at Port of Rauma loading and unloading trucks; unauditioned.
- [Rotterdam harbour area ambience](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Algemene_sfeer_van_het_havengebied_Rotterdam_-_SoundCloud_-_GvNL-Steim_Workshop.ogg): Robin Koek; CC BY 3.0. Source describes light water movement, birds and distant harbour activity; unauditioned.

## Rejected or deferred

- [WWS Warningsignal](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WWS_Warningsignal.ogg): rights are documented as CC BY 4.0, but it is a Swedish public warning signal test in a pedestrian street, not a terminal/equipment warning. Do not use it to imply crane or vehicle warning behaviour. It may inform comparison only.
- Domestic refrigerator, general compressor and industrial refrigeration sounds found in searches are not evidence of a shipping reefer unit. Do not promote them to reefer assets without clear provenance as a container refrigeration unit. No suitable item-level shipping reefer sound source has been verified so far.
- [Container-port ambience by klankbeeld on Freesound](https://freesound.org/people/klankbeeld/sounds/321871/) is a highly relevant source lead and its page states CC BY 4.0. It describes crane/container handling and high beeps, but download is account-gated (“Login to download”). No access control was bypassed and no file was obtained; revisit through ordinary account access if available.
- A generated/synthesised general alarm file is excluded because the project requires real recordings, even where the signal pattern itself is factual.

## Operational guidance

Warning sounds must be tied to the equipment and operating context; one generic alarm should not be played for every event. As a United States marine-terminal example, OSHA says rail-mounted cranes must have an effective travel warning device used to warn employees in the path of a moving crane ([29 CFR 1917.45](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1917/1917.45)). OSHA's marine-terminal safety publication recommends maintenance and operation of horns, backup alarms and warning devices on terminal vehicles ([OSHA 3337](https://obis.osha.gov/Publications/3337-07-2007-English-07192007.html)). These sources are not a universal rule set for all ports or jurisdictions. Local procedures and the specific machine determine whether a warning is appropriate.
