# Wikimedia Commons / Work With Sounds port audio candidates

Checked 2026-10-31. This is a source-page and metadata inventory, not a listening review. I checked Commons file descriptions, creator/recordist credits, file-level licence templates, and Commons `imageinfo` metadata. No audio was downloaded or auditioned for this note. Ogg durations and formats below come from Commons file metadata; item pages and API links are included per item. “Authentic” means the source documents a real recording and identifies its real equipment or place. It does not mean the recording has been listened to or validated acoustically.

100 unique, item-level verified **direct terminal-equipment** candidates are not realistically available from the material located. The branch separately adds 100 item-level licensed port/harbour recordings from the Aporee field-recording archive. Eight item descriptions mention cargo, terminal, ferry, or vessel operations; 92 are port/harbour soundscapes. Those additions remain unauditioned and are not promoted to isolated equipment events. Commons searches for Work With Sounds plus port, harbour, container, crane, dock, and ship terms surfaced a small group of real candidates; broader searches returned many false matches, non-audio files, generic factory machines, and unrelated ships. The strongest distinct set is four port-terminal equipment recordings, with a few port/shipyard adjacent soundscapes and support-equipment leads. The candidate list below is deliberately short so the relevance labels stay honest.

## Exclusions and limits

- [WWS Adjusting a drydock](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WWS_Adjustingadrydock.ogg) is a real floating-drydock recording, but the item description says it starts with a spoken explanation in Swedish. Excluded under the no-speech criterion.
- [WWS Sandblasting a ship hull](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WWS_Sandblastingashiphull.ogg) and [WWS Welding on a ship in a floating drydock](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WWS_Weldingonshipinfloatingdrydock.ogg) are authentic shipyard maintenance soundscapes, not port/terminal handling equipment. They are possible future shipyard ambience leads only.
- [WWS Fishing vessel](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:WWS_Fishingvessel.ogg) is an old fishing boat engine recording with long duration markers and engine activity, not port equipment.
- General bridge cranes, scrap handling, compressors, refrigerator sounds, warning tones, and factory machinery were not promoted unless an item page connected them to a real port/terminal setting. No synthetic alarms or speech/pronunciation recordings are included.
- The container-port Freesound lead [BCTN terminal, Den Bosch](https://freesound.org/people/klankbeeld/sounds/321871/) is outside Commons/WWS. Its source page describes container/crane handling and high beeps and indicates CC BY 4.0, but download is login-gated; it is not counted as a verified playable candidate here.
- These are source metadata checks only. They do not establish that a useful isolated event is audible, that a loop is clean, or that recording quality suits a release. Listen before mapping events or preparing derivatives. CC BY requires attribution; the CC BY-SA ship-arrival item carries additional share-alike obligations.
